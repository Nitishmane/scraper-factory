Self-healing web scrapers, as a factory

Scrapers rot. This treats it as an on-call problem.

The Scraper Factory reads a week and a half of TV-guide data across Philo, YouTube TV, and Sling, ranks the top 10 shows and top 10 movies by TMDB popularity, and gives you one click to record each title on Philo. When a scraper drifts, a repair loop fixes it — but a deterministic inspector with a frozen checklist decides whether the fix is real.

Explain it like I'm 10

Imagine a robot factory that reads the TV guide every day, picks the twenty best things to watch, and puts them on a poster you can tap. Sometimes the TV-guide website changes its shape and a reading-robot gets confused. So another robot tries to fix the confused one. But before anyone trusts the fix, a strict inspector checks the repaired robot against an answer sheet that never changes. No matching answer sheet? No promotion.

📺ReadRobots read the guide, one channel page at a time
🏆PickScore every title, keep the top 10 shows + 10 movies
📋ShowPut them on a poster with a Record button
🔧FixWhen a robot breaks, another robot repairs it
🕵️InspectAn inspector checks the fix against a frozen answer sheet

The one rule: the fixer can be a clever robot, but the inspector is a plain checklist you can trust every single time. Judgment goes to the agent. Proof stays deterministic.

The thesis, for grown-ups

Web scrapers are fragile by nature: a guide site ships a redesign, selectors stop matching, and the data either stops arriving or — worse — arrives subtly wrong. The Scraper Factory treats that as a reliability problem with an on-call rotation. Scrapers are catalogued services with health states. Every run is traced. Drift is an alert. Repair is an automated loop that must prove itself against a frozen fixture before its output is trusted, and escalates to a human when it can't.

Built on Port (control plane: catalog, actions, automations, scorecards, dashboards, AI agents), Bright Data Scraper Studio (the scrapers, plus self-healing), SigNoz (traces, metrics, logs), and TMDB (ranking signal).
